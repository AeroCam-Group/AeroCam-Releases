# 2. Connect Your Camera

AeroCam has four connection methods. You can set up more than one, and the app automatically uses whichever one is available. The four tiles on Home (Wi‑Fi, USB, Bluetooth, FTP) show their current status.

> Not sure how to connect? Tap **ⓘ** next to the connection status on Home, or at the top right of each step in "Add camera", to open a visual guide (one page each for phone hotspot, same Wi‑Fi, One-tap connect, USB, Bluetooth, and FTP) with screenshots for both phone and camera, plus a troubleshooting checklist at the end.

## How to Choose

| What you want | Best option |
|---|---|
| Receive photos even when the camera is turned off, hands-free | **Bluetooth** (smaller JPEGs, free) |
| Outdoors, no hotspot needed, want full-res files, gallery, and remote shooting | **One-tap connect** (Pro) |
| Instant auto-transfer right after every shot, fastest wireless speed | **[FTP Receive](08-ftp.md)** (Pro, requires hotspot) |
| Most reliable connection, video monitor, long remote sessions | **USB cable** (manual pick & download is free) |
| At home or in a studio | **Same Wi‑Fi** (Pro) |

## Speed Reference (Tested on Nikon Zf + Pixel 10 Pro)

| Method | One RAW file (~18 MB) |
|---|---|
| FTP receive | ~1 sec (~17 MB/s) |
| Wi‑Fi gallery download | ~1.3 sec (~13 MB/s) |
| USB | Usually faster than Wi‑Fi, depending on cable (~35 MB/s on a USB 2.0 cable) |
| Bluetooth | Smaller JPEG (~2 MB), 15–20 sec per photo |

## Status Lights on Home
The Wi‑Fi, USB, Bluetooth, and FTP tiles change color based on status (gray when not connected):

| Color | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 🟢 Green | Connected or ready (pale green on Bluetooth = standing by for Send while off) |
| 🔵 Blue | Transferring |
| 🟠 Orange | Connecting (blinking), or needs attention (permission, stalled, error) |
| 🔴 Red | Phone's Wi‑Fi or Bluetooth is turned off |

## Bluetooth (Free, Replaces SnapBridge)

### Pairing
1. On the camera: **MENU → Network menu → Connect to smart device → Pairing (Bluetooth) → Start pairing**.
2. If your camera was paired with SnapBridge, delete the pairing in SnapBridge first and forget the camera in your phone's Bluetooth settings (two apps cannot be paired to the same camera at once).
3. In AeroCam: Home → "Bluetooth" → **Pair camera** → Start. Both phone and camera show the same code — confirm on both. The camera shows your phone as `AeroCam_<phone model>`.

📷 Screenshot: Bluetooth tile

### After Pairing
Once paired, Bluetooth stays active until you unpair (which asks for confirmation first).
- Normally, AeroCam only **listens** in the background and will not wake a sleeping camera.
- **When you turn the camera on**: AeroCam connects briefly to sync the clock and your phone's GPS location (so the camera embeds location in new photos), then disconnects.
- **When the camera has photos to send**: Enable **Send while off** in the camera's "Connect to smart device" menu. The camera automatically connects over Bluetooth to send photos, even after you turn it off.
- Received photos are ~2 MB JPEGs generated by the camera (RAW shots are converted to JPEG by the camera first), taking about 15–20 seconds each. Use Wi‑Fi, USB, or FTP for original files.
- To pull photos right away, tap **Receive now** on the Bluetooth tile.
- Service resumes automatically after toggling Bluetooth, rebooting your phone, or updating the app. While waiting, Android requires a low-priority persistent notification, which you can hide in system settings.

### Bluetooth Tile States
🟢 Camera nearby · On / 🟢 Pale green: Standby (Send while off ready) / ⚪ Camera not seen (shows last seen time) / 🔵 Transferring / 🟠 May be stalled / 🔴 Phone Bluetooth is off.

Because Bluetooth does not stay continuously connected, this tile shows whether the camera is heard nearby and what it is doing right now.

### If Transfer Stalls
- If no data arrives for 30 seconds during a transfer, the app disconnects automatically and the camera retries later; after 20 seconds of inactivity, the tile turns yellow ("May be stalled").
- **Disconnect and reconnect**: drops only the current transfer session.
- **⋮ → Restart Bluetooth service**: restarts the entire background Bluetooth service while keeping your pairing.
- When shooting RAW, the camera must convert each file to JPEG before sending. Operating the camera during transfer (half-pressing the shutter or taking a photo) cancels the transfer. It works best if you simply turn the camera off after shooting and let it send in the background.

## Wi‑Fi (Pro): Three Ways to Connect

| Method | How it works | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **One-tap connect** (recommended) | Uses Bluetooth to tell the camera to turn on its own Wi‑Fi, then connects automatically | Outdoors, when you don't want to turn on a hotspot |
| **Phone hotspot** (recommended in guide) | Camera connects to your phone's mobile hotspot | When One-tap connect is unstable, or when using FTP or shooting without a card |
| **Same Wi‑Fi** | Both phone and camera connect to the same router | At home or in a studio |

### One-tap Connect
1. Complete **Bluetooth pairing** first.
2. On Home, tap Wi‑Fi → "One-tap connect".
3. The first time, enter your camera's Wi‑Fi **password** (the network name, like `NIKON_…`, is filled in automatically). It is stored only on your phone.
4. When Android asks to allow the Wi‑Fi connection, tap Allow.
5. Once "Connected to camera Wi‑Fi" appears, gallery, remote shooting, and auto receive all use this link. Tap "Disconnect" to return to your normal Wi‑Fi.

If gallery or remote shooting isn't connected yet, you will also see a "Connect via camera Wi‑Fi" button right on screen.

> - While connected to the camera's Wi‑Fi, your phone uses mobile data for internet access.
> - One-tap connect **cannot be used with FTP**, and **does not support shooting without a card** (use a phone hotspot or same Wi‑Fi when there is no card in the camera).

📷 Screenshot: One-tap connect card and connected state

### Phone Hotspot / Same Wi‑Fi (First-time Pairing)
1. On the camera: **MENU → Network menu → Connect to PC**, and create a network profile (connecting to your phone's hotspot or home Wi‑Fi).
   - Newer models (with "Connection type" in the menu): set "Connection type" to **Image transfer**.
   - Earlier models: simply select **Connect to PC**.
2. In AeroCam: **Home → "+" Add camera**, and pick the connection method (Same Wi‑Fi or Phone hotspot). The app checks your network and searches for the camera automatically; you can also enter its IP manually.
3. The camera screen displays a **4-digit PIN**. Enter it on your phone, then press OK on the camera.
4. Once paired, it is saved as a camera profile so you can connect directly next time. You can save multiple cameras and multiple networks. You **do not need to re-pair** after app updates, and AeroCam finds the camera even if its IP address changes.

> If the camera says "Already paired with another device", that camera network profile is used by another app. Create a new profile on the camera and pair again.

📷 Screenshot: Add camera wizard and PIN entry

### Pre-connection Checks
- When you tap "Start receiving" or open Camera roll / Remote, AeroCam checks your network first. If the profile uses "Phone hotspot" while the hotspot is off, or "Same Wi‑Fi" while your phone isn't connected to it, a yellow banner appears (e.g., "Please turn on your phone hotspot first") with quick buttons for "Hotspot settings" and "Wi‑Fi settings". Once enabled, return to AeroCam and the banner clears.
- If the camera cannot be found on Wi‑Fi or hotspot, Home shows "Camera not found on network" (usually because "Connect to PC" is not active on the camera) with a "View connection guide" button.

### Camera "Connection type"
On newer models (Z9, Z8, Z6III, Zf, Z5II, Z50II…): **MENU → Network menu → Connect to PC → Connection type**
- **Image transfer**: for auto receive and browsing the camera SD card.
- **Camera control**: for remote shooting and shooting without a card.

Switching modes briefly disconnects the camera's Wi‑Fi; AeroCam reconnects automatically without re-pairing. If you open Remote while the camera is still in "Image transfer", the app shows the steps to switch.

## USB Cable
Faster and more reliable than Wi‑Fi. **No pairing required**, and you don't need to add the camera first.
1. Set your camera's USB option to **MTP/PTP** (not "iPhone" or "UVC/UAC").
2. Use a **data-capable** USB‑C cable (some cables are charge-only). A USB 3 cable rated 5Gbps/10Gbps gives the best speed.
3. On Home, the USB tile shows "Z f detected · Access permission needed" → tap "Grant access" and confirm the Android prompt once.

### USB Tile Colors
| Color | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ⚪ Gray | No camera detected (or cable is charge-only) |
| 🟠 Orange | Camera detected; access permission needed |
| 🔴 Red | Camera USB setting is not MTP/PTP, permission denied, or test failed (shows reason) |
| 🟢 Green | Connected over USB |

### While Connected over USB
- Camera roll, Remote, and auto receive **automatically switch to USB**; unplugging switches back to Wi‑Fi. Plugging in during Wi‑Fi remote shooting also switches to USB seamlessly (except while interval timer, bracketing, or Bulb exposure is running).
- **USB receive** (Pro): Tap "Start receiving" on the USB tile to save newly shot photos and photos sent via "Send to PC" on the camera. Enabled automatically on plug-in by default (**Settings → Shooting → "Auto-start receiving when USB cable is plugged in"**).
- **Clock sync**: Syncs the camera clock on plug-in (only adjusts if off by more than 2 seconds; asks first if off by more than 1 day).
- **Keep awake**: Sends a tiny keep-alive signal every 10 seconds while Camera roll or Remote is open so the camera doesn't sleep; stops after 30 minutes of inactivity or when the camera battery is low.
- Your phone may supply power to the camera over USB — consider using a power bank for long sessions.
- **USB test** (⋮ at the top right of the USB tile): tests connection, camera info, memory cards, live view frame rate, and read speed.
